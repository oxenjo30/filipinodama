## Target 2: FilipiKnow (filipiknow.net)

**Why them:** FilipiKnow is a long-form Filipino reference site whose article ["7 Traditional Filipino Games You've Probably Never Heard Of"](https://filipiknow.net/traditional-filipino-games/) (verified by direct fetch 2026-07-19) covers Sangkayaw, Bati-Cobra, Lawin at Sisiw, Juego de Anillo and others — but **does not mention dama at all**. Their style is researched listicles that cite institutions like the NCCA, so a well-sourced dama history/rules resource fits their editorial bar.

**Story angle:** The gap in their coverage — dama is arguably the most-played traditional Filipino board game (plazas, barbershops, bottle-cap boards) yet missing from their traditional-games article; offer FilipinoDama's culture and rules pages as reference material, or offer to help with a dedicated dama piece.

**Pitch email:**

It has genuinely distinct rules — men capture backward as well as forward, captures are mandatory with a maximum-capture rule, and a crowned piece becomes a "flying" dama that slides the whole diagonal.
>
> I'm John, owner of FilipinoDama.com, where I've documented the full rules (in English and Tagalog) and the game's history, plus a free browser version so readers can try it. If you ever cover dama — as an update to that article or a standalone piece — feel free to use the site as a reference or point readers there to play. I'm also happy to fact-check or contribute detail; the rule set is something I've verified move by move.

> Salamat po,
> John
