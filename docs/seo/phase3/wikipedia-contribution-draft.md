## 2. Sources found (independent; none affiliated with filipinodama.com)

### Verified directly during this research

**S1. NCCA / Philippine Cultural Education Program — "dáma" entry, CulturEd portal**
URL: https://philippineculturaleducation.com.ph/dama/
Publisher: National Commission for Culture and the Arts (Philippine government cultural agency), CulturEd / Philippine Cultural Education Program. The entry is credited to *Sagisag Kultura* (Vol. 1), ed. Virgilio S. Almario, NCCA, 2015.
Verified content (page is in Filipino; fetched 2026-07-19): describes dáma as a Filipino game akin to chess/checkers whose object is to eliminate the opponent's pieces; traces the game's European origin (medieval "ferses" → French "dames" popular by the 16th century → eventually reaching the Philippines); notes pieces are fashioned from *"kawayan, bato, o takip ng bote"* (bamboo, stone, or bottle caps); pieces move diagonally; reaching the far row grants extended movement; also documents a reversed "losing" variant called **perdegána**.
Supports: dama as Philippine tradition; European origin (the entry as read traces ferses → French ''dames'' → Philippines and does not itself mention Spanish transmission — do not cite it for "Spanish"); improvised everyday materials (bottle caps); reversed "losing" (giveaway) variant.

**S2. The Philippine Star — "DepEd promotes 'Damath'", 20 February 2010 (staff report, no byline)**
URL: https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2010/02/20/550971/deped-promotes-damath
Verified content (fetched 2026-07-19), exact quotes:
- *"'Damath,' coined from the Pinoy checkerboard game 'dama' and mathematics, is now being promoted by the Department of Education (DepEd) as a Filipino way of learning math."*
- *"The practical approach to contextualized teaching and learning math is the brainchild of 1981 presidential merit medal awardee teacher Jesus Huenda. As a public high school teacher in Sorsogon, Huenda always thought of ways to optimize his talents to help others."*
- Also mentions Huenda's *"pierdi-gana"* board game "scidama" (reversed-objective variant — cross-confirms S1's perdegána tradition).
Supports: Damath invention (Huenda, Sorsogon), DepEd promotion, dama described as "the Pinoy checkerboard game" by a national newspaper.

```wikitext
===Dama===
[[File:Dama (IMG 7077).jpg|thumb|Dama]]

Dama is a traditional Filipino [[board game]] of the [[draughts]] (checkers) family, in which players capture the opponent's pieces by leaping over them.<ref name="ncca-dama" /> The game is of European origin — a lineage that traces to European draughts, which eventually reached the Philippines.<ref name="ncca-dama" /> In folk play, pieces are improvised from everyday materials such as bamboo, stone, or bottle caps.<ref name="ncca-dama" /> A reversed "losing" variant called ''perdegána'' is also traditionally played.<ref name="ncca-dama" />

The American ethnographer [[Stewart Culin]] documented dama as played in the Philippines in 1900.<ref name="culin1900" /> In the form he recorded, the board consists of a grid of points connected by diagonal lines, with twelve pieces per player positioned on the three rows closest to each player.<ref name="culin1900" /> Players take turns moving a piece forward to an empty adjacent point along the lines, and capture an opponent's piece by hopping over it to an empty point beyond it. Multiple captures are allowed. When a piece reaches the far edge of the board it becomes a king (''dama''), which may move any distance diagonally and capture any piece it leaps over. The first player to capture all of the opponent's pieces wins.<ref name="culin1900" />

====Damath====
{{Main|Damath}}
Damath is an educational variant of dama that combines the game with [[mathematics]] drills.<ref name="philstar2010" /> It was devised by Jesus Huenda, a public high school teacher in [[Sorsogon]], and has been promoted by the [[Department of Education (Philippines)|Department of Education]] as a Filipino way of learning mathematics; the name is a [[portmanteau]] of ''dama'' and ''mathematics''.<ref name="philstar2010" /> Huenda also devised a reversed-objective (''pierdi-gana'') game called Scidama.<ref name="philstar2010" />
```
